Project BlueConnect: a lifeline for fishermen beyond the mobile network

A Visakhapatnam innovator is building a low-cost communication and safety system that lets small fishing boats send SOS alerts, live GPS locations and even free custom text messages from the deep sea — where no mobile network exists.

S
Someswara (Sri Somesh) Inventor & Builder · Visakhapatnam, India
● Prototype working

Every single morning, more than four million small-scale fishermen push their motorised boats into the sea along India's 7,500-kilometre coastline. They are the backbone of the country's blue economy. But roughly five kilometres from the shore, a terrifying reality sets in: the cellular signal drops to zero. If an outboard motor fails, a sudden storm hits, or a boat drifts toward a maritime boundary — the fisherman simply vanishes. He cannot call for help. His family waits on the shore in agony.

A gap nobody has filled

The reason this problem persists is economics, not technology. Commercial satellite terminals cost upwards of ₹50,000, plus heavy monthly subscription fees — far beyond the reach of artisanal fishing families. Traditional VHF marine radios require strict licensing, suffer from audio static, and cannot transmit automatic GPS locations. The people who need connectivity the most are exactly the ones priced out of it.

"The moment their boats cross the five-kilometre mark, they are completely blind. We refused to accept that answer." — Someswara, inventor of Project BlueConnect

The invention

Project BlueConnect (also called Rakshak Marine) pairs a small, rugged, waterproof unit on the boat with a base station on the shore, linked over long-range, licence-free legal radio spectrum. The target cost is under ₹5,000 per boat — with zero monthly charges. No telecom operator, no SIM card, no subscription is involved.

When a fisherman is in trouble, he presses a single rugged button. Within moments, the shore station knows the exact boat identity, the type of emergency, and the precise GPS coordinates — dropped as a live pin on a map, ready for a rescue vessel to deploy.

The surprise: free text messages from the fisherman's own phone

Real emergencies are messy — no pre-made button can say "crew member has a high fever, need a doctor at the dock." So BlueConnect does something unusual: the fisherman's ordinary smartphone — in flight mode, with no signal at all — connects locally to the boat unit and becomes a free keyboard and screen. He types any message he wants, hits send, and it travels across kilometres of open ocean to the shore operator. Entirely free.

Where the project stands

Project BlueConnect — at a glance

  • Status: fully functional, field-tested prototype — boat unit + shore station with live map dashboard
  • Range: engineered for a consistent 20–30 km off the coast
  • Cost class: under ₹5,000 per boat · ₹0 monthly
  • Compliance: licence-free legal spectrum; emergency VHF Channel 16 left completely clear
  • For authorities: automatic fleet logging, boundary-crossing prevention, coastal security support

The system has been demonstrated live: a button press on the boat unit appearing as a flashing alert with exact coordinates on the shore map within moments, and a custom phone-typed message intercepted on shore in under two seconds — all without a single rupee paid to a telecom company.
